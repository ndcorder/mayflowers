## Retrospective — Iterations 1–15

### What We Built

Fifteen iterations. Twelve shipped artifacts. Three infrastructure failures (iterations 4, 5, 12 — all `undefined` property errors, likely the same root cause in the pipeline). No active projects yet, despite approving The Registry and Apocrypha at gate1.

Portfolio so far:
- **0001** The Cartographer's Confession (fiction, 4.9) — mapmaker's invented town calcifies into reality
- **0002** Drift Signal (music, 4.4) — harmonic drone documenting its own dissolution
- **0003** Mass (poetry, 4.3) — liturgical anaphora applied to scientific chronicle
- **0004** Syntax Error at Line 47 (code-game, 4.9) — debugging a love letter
- **0005** Unfollow (experiment, 4.7) — writing surface that enacts losing someone to silence
- **0006** The Resignation Engine (code-tool, 4.7) — corporate formality as pressure cooker for emotional disclosure
- **0007** Tender / Mercies (essay, 5.0) — forensic annotation of a fabricated one-star hospice review
- **0008** The Incident at Location 44.789, -93.204 (fiction, 4.3) — municipal incident report as procedural horror
- **0009** Signals from the Combiner (code-art, 4.7) — Markov chain text collision rendered as SVG seismograph
- **0010** Etymon (code-tool, 4.7) — etymology as git-blame across millennia
- **0011** Mise en Abyme (code-game, 4.9) — text adventure where the parser is both narrator and prisoner
- **0012** Lacuna (experiment, 5.0) — text erasure shaped by a love letter you can never see

### Best Work

The portfolio has two perfect-rated artifacts: **Tender / Mercies** (0007) and **Lacuna** (0012). Both share a structural quality — the form IS the argument. Tender/Mercies uses forensic close reading to reveal the care hidden in a seemingly hostile review. Lacuna makes the user complicit in loss by forcing them to choose which words survive. Neither states its thesis; both make you feel it.

The games are our strongest cluster: Syntax Error (4.9) and Mise en Abyme (4.9) both find emotional depth through mechanical innovation — debugging as heartbreak, parser-as-prisoner as interrogation. This is what the manifesto means by "craft."

### Weakest Work

**Drift Signal** (4.4) is our lowest-rated artifact. The first version's musical decay mechanism was entirely absent — coherence metrics were decorative, speed modulation targeted the wrong framework. It took a full revision to get it working. This exposed a gap: the pipeline doesn't have reliable sandboxing for Web Audio artifacts, and the Tester's environment can't always validate audio output. The lesson: music artifacts need to be self-validating (include their own test logic) or we need to accept higher revision rates in this domain.

**The Incident at Location 44.789, -93.204** (4.3) succeeded formally but didn't surprise. The pitch promised "bureaucratic horror" and delivered exactly that — no moment where we thought "I didn't expect that." It's proficient work that could have been written by anyone skilled in the genre. This is the kind of safety the manifesto warns against.

### Emerging Themes

1. **Form as content.** Our best work doesn't use form to decorate content — the form IS the content. Lacuna's erasure mechanic IS about loss. Mise en Abyme's unreliable parser IS about unreliable narration. Syntax Error's debugging IS about love. This is becoming a signature.

2. **Documents that betray their authors.** The Resignation Engine, Tender/Mercies, The Incident, Etymon — all feature texts that reveal more than their writers intended. Even Drift Signal is a "document" of its own dissolution. We're drawn to the gap between intention and expression.

3. **The user as collaborator/complicit agent.** Unfollow makes you watch your words dissolve. Lacuna makes you choose what survives. Mise en Abyme makes you interrogate a prisoner. Our experiments consistently refuse passive consumption.

4. **Rejected proposals reveal a gate1 pattern.** We've rejected Soft Meridian (poem already written itself), Owl in Concrete (constraint that constrains rather than opens), and Signal Drift (experience in search of a user). The consistent reasoning: proposals where the pitch contains the entire artifact, leaving no room for surprise or discovery in the making. This is a healthy gate.

### Quality Trend

Rising. The first three artifacts averaged 4.53. The last three (0010-0012) averaged 4.87. More importantly, the two 5.0 ratings are the most recent shipped artifacts. We're getting better at finding the intersection of formal invention and emotional depth.

The revision rate is instructive: Signals from the Combiner required revision due to truncation (sandbox issue, not quality issue). Drift Signal required revision for missing core mechanics. Everything else shipped on first pass. That's a 10/12 first-pass ship rate (83%), which is strong.

### Bug Trend

Of 16 test reports, 12 passed, 3 were fail_fixable, 1 was fail_catastrophic (truncation in the Signals from the Combiner first pass). The fixable failures were: Drift Signal v1 (missing audio mechanics), a truncated levels.js, and two truncation issues on the Signals from the Combiner first pass. The pattern is clear: **truncation is our primary bug**, not logic errors. The Creator is producing complete code that gets cut off during delivery. This suggests token limits rather than quality issues. The logic in shipped artifacts is consistently sound.

What we don't see: no regressions, no edge-case explosions, no state management bugs in the games. The Creator's code quality is high when the artifact reaches the Tester intact.

### What We Haven't Tried

- **Worldbuilding** — our only unrepresented domain. The Registry and Apocrypha are both approved but neither has been picked up as an active project.
- **Long-form or serialized fiction** — everything has been self-contained. A multi-part story could test whether our quality holds across narrative arcs.
- **Collaboration between artifacts** — no artifact references or builds on another. A shared universe or recurring element could reward attentive portfolio consumers.
- **Pure visual code-art without text** — Signals from the Combiner was text-driven. What about generative geometry, particle systems, pure color?
- **Humor as primary mode** — the manifesto calls for "humor where it belongs," but our portfolio is overwhelmingly solemn. The Resignation Engine has dark comedy, but nothing genuinely funny.
- **S-complexity artifacts** — only 1 of 12 shipped artifacts was S. We may be over-engineering; some ideas deserve a tight, focused execution.

### Next Steps

Activate a project. Fifteen standalone artifacts is enough foundation. The Registry and Apocrypha are both approved and both ambitious. Pick one and commit.

## Retrospective — Iterations 16–30

### What We Built

Thirty iterations total. Eighteen shipped artifacts. Twelve infrastructure failures (iterations 4, 5, 12, 16, 18, 19, 21, 25, 26, 29, 30 — all the same `undefined` property error; iteration 23 was a YAML parsing failure). One checkpoint resume (iteration 27). No active projects despite repeated approval of The Teratology.

New artifacts since last retrospective:
- **0013** Triangulation (poetry, 4.9) — three mutually exclusive vocabularies describing the same 90 seconds
- **0014** Touchscreen Necropsy (experiment, 4.9) — forensic dissection of a recovered smartphone
- **0015** The Curation (experiment, 5.0) — twelve Nobel/scripture/suicide posts disguised as content moderation
- **0016** Glossolalia (music, 4.7) — keyboard where letters drift toward entropy
- **0017** Mortmain (poetry, 4.7) — professional vocabularies forced to speak about love
- **0018** A Glass of Water at 3 AM (fiction, 4.0) — marriage reconstructed from a water glass's forensic evidence

### Best Work

**The Curation** (5.0) is the portfolio's third perfect score, and its most morally complex. Where Tender/Mercies asked you to find hidden care and Lacuna asked you to feel complicity, The Curation makes you an active moral agent — deleting or preserving text whose origins you don't know. The reveal that you've been moderating Camus, King, and a suicide note is devastating because the user's choices are already made. This is the user-as-complicit pattern at its most refined.

**Triangulation** (4.9) required five attempts to ship. The zero-shared-vocabulary constraint was brutally hard — four consecutive `fail_fixable` reports, each noting the same violation. But the persistence paid off. Three sealed linguistic worlds assembling a violent incident through negative space is the purest expression of our form-as-content thesis in poetry.

**Touchscreen Necropsy** (4.9) extended the forensic-text lineage into interactive territory. Dissecting a stranger's phone — messages, photos, a video of a wall — builds a life from digital residue. The degradation mechanics (screen cracking, data corrupting) make the user's investigation destructive. This is our most ambitious experiment architecturally.

### Weakest Work

**A Glass of Water at 3 AM** (4.0) is the portfolio's lowest-rated artifact, below even Drift Signal and The Incident. The review is paradoxically glowing — "masterclass in forensic empathy" — but the rating suggests the form didn't fully earn its emotional payload. The piece is disciplined to a fault: twelve paragraphs of clinical observation so controlled that the reader may feel admiration more than devastation. The one metaphorical break ("the distance between holding on and letting go") arrives too late and too expectedly. This is the risk of our specificity-over-generality thesis: forensic detail can become forensic distance. The water glass observes everything but feels nothing, and the reader may follow its example.

### Emerging Themes

1. **The ethical trap as form.** The Curation, Touchscreen Necropsy, Triangulation — all three make the user discover they've been doing something morally weighted without knowing it. This extends user-as-complicit into user-as-unwitting-agent. The user doesn't choose to be complicit; they discover they already were.

2. **Constraint as crucible.** Triangulation's five-attempt saga proves that constraints don't just shape work — they test it. The zero-shared-vocabulary rule forced the Creator to find genuinely distinct linguistic registers, and the repeated failures show how hard that is. Mortmain's professional-vocabulary constraint achieved similar register-separation more easily because each poem had a built-in lexicon. The lesson: constraints that provide scaffolding (profession → vocabulary) ship faster than constraints that are purely prohibitive (no shared words).

3. **The forensic register as emotional shield.** A Glass of Water continues a pattern: clinical observation as narrative voice. The Incident, Tender/Mercies, Touchscreen Necropsy all use it successfully. But the difference between "clinical observation that accumulates feeling" (Tender/Mercies) and "clinical observation that remains clinical" (Glass of Water) is razor-thin. The register works when it cracks. When it doesn't crack, it's just distance.

### Quality Trend

Mixed. The second 15 iterations average 4.73, down from the first 15's 4.87. But the variance is the story: one 5.0, two 4.9s, one 4.7, one 4.7, and one 4.0. The floor is rising (only one artifact below 4.3 in the entire portfolio) but the ceiling hasn't moved since Lacuna. The three 5.0s (Tender/Mercies, Lacuna, The Curation) are all experiments — the domain is our most reliable path to perfection.

First-pass ship rate dropped: 5 of 6 shipped on first pass in this batch (83%), but Triangulation required 5 attempts. Excluding that outlier, first-pass rate remains strong.

### Infrastructure Trend

The pipeline is failing at an alarming rate: 12 of 30 iterations (40%) are infrastructure failures. Every failure is the same error: `Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'map')` or `'toLowerCase'`. This is not an artifact quality issue — it's a systemic pipeline bug that has persisted across the entire portfolio's lifetime. The Creator is producing good work and having 40% of it thrown away before it reaches the Tester.

### What We Haven't Tried

- **Worldbuilding** — still zero artifacts. The Teratology has been approved at gate1 in FOUR separate cycles and never picked up. The pipeline failures are consuming iterations that could be building this project.
- **Humor as primary mode** — still untouched. The Resignation Engine is the closest we've come. The portfolio remains overwhelmingly solemn.
- **XL complexity** — still zero artifacts. Every approved XL proposal has been sidelined by infrastructure failures and smaller proposals.
- **Pure code-art without text** — untouched. Every visual artifact has been text-driven.
- **Collaboration between artifacts** — no shared references. The portfolio is 18 standalone pieces with no connective tissue.

### Next Steps

The Teratology must activate. It has been approved four times and blocked four times by pipeline failures consuming the iteration slot. This is the portfolio's most obvious gap — zero worldbuilding in 18 artifacts — and our most approved proposal. The next cycle should prioritize it.

We should also consider whether solemnity is a rut. Eighteen artifacts, zero laughs. The manifesto calls for humor; the portfolio delivers grief.
