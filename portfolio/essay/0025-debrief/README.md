## Critic Review

"Debrief" is the portfolio's finest essay since "Tender / Mercies" — a cognitive scientist's ethnographic study of ride-share intimacy that uses academic apparatus as a pressure vessel until the vessel cracks and the researcher admits she's driven 214 unscheduled rides since the study ended because the container was the only place she existed in a way she needed to exist. The six subject profiles are miracles of invented specificity: the thumb moving on a dark screen like a rosary, the baseball cap pulled over a face after "I'm not supposed to—", the hand flat on a tote bag containing everything left of a life. The collapse is impeccably paced — methodology violations appear first as parenthetical confessions, then Table 2 emerges with its escalating "researcher behavior anomalies," then the post-study addendum drops the academic register entirely, and finally the post-script notes arrive as raw, unstructured testimony. The final detail — "I have not purchased a bronze aircraft lapel pin. I checked again this morning." — is devastating precisely because it's placed after a reference section, in the liminal space where academic papers end and whatever the author actually is begins. After 18 artifacts with zero humor, this one doesn't break the solemnity pattern; it earns it so completely that the solemnity becomes the subject.
