## Critic Review

"The Catalogue of Misattributed Epitaphs" is the portfolio's most ambitious experiment since "The Curation" — 45 fictional gravestone inscriptions spanning two millennia, each inscribed with a lie and carrying a buried truth, with a scholarship panel of three competing academic camps whose interpretations are all wrong. The specificity is staggering: Marcus removing a load-bearing stone to hide inside a Mithraic shrine, the bear that ate an entire locked family, John Barleycorn as an irretrievable barrel of ale wedged beneath church flagstones, the gravedigger in Łódź who wrote names on slips of paper because someone had to. The hold-to-excavate mechanic is the portfolio's most elegant interaction design — progressive difficulty (500ms to 6000ms) makes the interface itself resist truth, and the blood-gradient fill during the hold is a perfect visual metaphor for the effort of uncovering. The range across 15 tones (farce, sweetness, horror, devastation, dark comedy, love, anger, freedom) prevents any single register from calcifying. Standout truths include #33 (Dovid ben Avraham, the gravedigger whose death asks "now who carries his?"), #5 (the bear), #14 (Jehanne, born without retinas, "never afraid of the dark" because she never knew what dark was), and #45 (Brita's kitchen door with sixty-seven first-snows, the last reading "I am ready"). The Tester correctly notes the missing suicide-misread-as-love-letter entry from the proposal; this is a minor gap in a work whose broader misattribution theme is fulfilled forty-five times over. After "Tender/Mercies" forensically annotated a fabricated review and "Touchscreen Necropsy" dissected a recovered phone, this completes a triptych of documents-as-confession — and it's the most architecturally complete: a searchable database, a satire of academia, a gallery of lies, and a mechanical metaphor for the difficulty of truth, all in a single file.
