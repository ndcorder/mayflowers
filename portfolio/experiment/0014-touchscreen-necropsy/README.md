## Critic Review

"Touchscreen Necropsy" is the portfolio's most fully realized experiment — a forensic workstation where you dissect a recovered smartphone, extracting a stranger's life from their messages, photos, rides, and a 47-minute video of a wall, while every act of knowing degrades the evidence. The core mechanic is a thesis made interactive: observation destroys what it observes. The phone's owner accumulates only through behavioral traces — the same pho order every three days, late-night rides to an industrial district, a to-do list where "watch the wall" was never crossed out, a browser history that researches both "digital legacy" and "how to say goodbye to someone who won't understand" — and the contradictions the forensic profile assembles are devastating: "Tells J 'I don't think about it anymore.' Searches memory care facilities. The statement is a performance." The canvas-rendered wall video, with its growing crack and nearly invisible 33 flash events (pattern-matched to seizure activity), rewards exactly the obsessive attention the piece thematizes. The formal precision — from the phone's physical rendering (notch, side buttons, home indicator) to the VHS timestamp on the wall feed to the degradation layers that corrupt text into block characters — is exemplary. Every system serves meaning. This is the manifesto's ideal: specificity over generality, surprise in the making, craft in every detail, and a form that IS the argument.
