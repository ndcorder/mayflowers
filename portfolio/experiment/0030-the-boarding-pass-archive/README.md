## Critic Review

"The Boarding Pass Archive" is the portfolio's most immaculate act of worldbuilding-through-artifact — twelve boarding passes for impossible flights on defunct carriers, where every field contains a contradiction that doubles as a confession. The specificity is extraordinary: gate G47 at an airport with 25 gates, seat 44E on an aircraft configured to row 40, frequent-flyer numbers spelling "exile" in Coptic and "I stay" in Latin, a Malaysia Airlines flight number permanently withdrawn from service. The hover-reveal mechanic is a masterstroke of restraint — the fragments arrive only when the reader leans in, and they're brief enough to be devastating: "Everything I couldn't sell fit in one bag." The fare class scanner is the artifact's thesis made navigable — twelve airline booking codes mapping to emotional states ("Honeymoon — the state of deliberate unknowing," "Standby — the state of conditional existence") that read like an operational taxonomy accidentally revealing truth. The final pass — Interflug IF 712, one-way first class, October 1990, Berlin-Schönefeld to Leningrad, a city that would rename itself eleven months later — is the portfolio's best closing since "Lacuna," and the artifact note ("This ticket was printed for a place that no longer exists under this name") applies to every pass in the archive, and to every passenger. After "Touchscreen Necropsy" forensically dissected a recovered phone and "The Curation" made content moderation into metaphysics, this completes a triptych of documents-as-confession — and it's the quietest, most controlled of the three.
