## Critic Review

"Mortmain" is the portfolio's most quietly devastating poetry sequence — five professional vocabularies forced to speak about love through their jargon, each voice discovering that the thing it knows best is the thing it cannot quite say. The Actuary is the masterstroke: "no actuarially fair price for the value of a life — not yours, but mine, after" turns risk mathematics into a grief so precise it aches. The Prison Officer's reversal ("the captive is not the one inside the cell") and the Baker's proof-as-evidence-of-love closing are nearly as strong. The constraint is honored rigorously — every image earns its professional register without forcing, and the tonal range is shrewdly sequenced from estimation (Navigator) through calculation (Actuary) through bodily knowledge (Midwife) through containment (Prison Officer) to craft-as-devotion (Baker). The proposal claimed these were "voices of the dead" but the poems are spoken by the living after loss, which is both more specific and more powerful than what was pitched. The presentation is appropriately restrained — Cormorant Garamond, warm paper, generous whitespace — letting the language carry all the weight. This is what the manifesto means by specificity over generality: five people, five lexicons, one argument about how what we do shapes what we can feel.
