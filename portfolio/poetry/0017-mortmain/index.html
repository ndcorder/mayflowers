mortmain

The Navigator

You were my fixed point when the currents

would not still. By dead reckoning I held

your longitude — an estimate, always an estimate,

the margin of error a fathom I could sound within.

Now my bearing is unmoored. The log reads

only elapsed. I am past the edge of the table

where the cartographer set down his pen

and said enough.

In the lee of your memory I find calm water.

An anchorage no windward fetch can reach.

The depth here, I cannot sound.

The Actuary

Force of mortality in year one was point-zero-three.

By year forty, compounded, actuarially

we should not still be — the probability

a rounding error I carried forward anyway,

treating each year's premium as an investment

in a policy that would never mature.

There is no line for this in the reserve table.

Insufficient data. The confidence interval

includes zero — so technically, within the model,

our variance is not significant. Technically,

you cannot prove what I carried

beyond the end of the projected period.

I modelled loss daily. This is not that.

This is a claim no policy was written to cover.

A liability I accept with no expectation of offset,

no amortization schedule,

no actuarially fair price

for the value of a life — not yours,

but mine, after.

The Midwife

Before crowning, there is the thinning —

cervix yielding, dilating, the slow consent of tissue

to an opening it was made for. This is the first thing

I learned: that the body knows the shape of what comes.

I have held the moment the lungs open — alveoli

unfolding like a map of a country that has always existed

but never been drawn. The first breath is a kind

of crying and also a kind of arriving.

I want to say: your pulse was a labor I could not attend.

Your heart, the atrium I could not suture closed.

What breaches in the end is not a dilation

but a rupture — the kind where bleeding

cannot be staunched by pressure.

I have delivered what I could. The vernix

of your presence wiped clean. The cord

between us — cut. And still: this is the language

I have — placenta, fontanel, quickening —

when what I mean is the moment of holding

which needs no clinical name at all.

The Prison Officer

The wing holds four hundred. My rounds

are every thirty minutes. I know the sound

of a lock engaging — deadbolt, mortise, slam —

and the particular silence after a key turns.

It is a silence that means something has been

correctly contained.

I contained myself from you. Put up the barrier

where required. Kept my count accurate. Logged

every incident of contact. The record shows

I was thorough. The record does not show

what I wanted.

Here is the truth the manual does not address:

the captive is not the one inside the cell.

The captive is the one who walks the perimeter,

checking the walls, ensuring they hold —

who knows that if the door opened

he would not leave.

I kept you behind glass. I patrolled

the boundary of what I could acknowledge.

I counted the minutes between us. They were

always standard interval.

They were never enough.

The Baker

First you make the sponge — yeast, water, flour —

and wait. Nothing happens. Then everything.

The dough rises because it has been given time

and warmth, and because someone believed it would.

I kneaded you into me. Worked the heel of my hand

until the gluten strands caught and held. Salt —

always salt, to make the flavor deeper.

You cannot rush bread. You cannot rush love.

You can only keep the room warm enough

that transformation becomes possible.

The crust forms in the shock of the oven.

A caramelizing. A hardness that protects

the soft interior. You taught me this:

that the toughened outside is not the loaf's betrayal

but its integrity — a shell that lets the crumb

stay tender.

The oven is cold now. The proof box, empty.

My hands still move in the old patterns —

fold, press, turn, fold — shaping bread

for a table I no longer set. This is the argument

my hands make against my ending:

that to have made something well is enough.

That to have made it for you

was a kind of proof.
