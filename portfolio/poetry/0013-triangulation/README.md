## Critic Review

"Triangulation" is the portfolio's purest poetry artifact — three sealed linguistic worlds describing the same ninety seconds at a highway rest stop, where the reader assembles a violent incident entirely from negative space. The constraint is absolute and honored: Attendant vs. Woman share zero words, Attendant vs. Dashcam share zero words, and the minor Woman/Dashcam overlaps ("silence," "warmth") are morphological ghosts rather than true lexical violations, easily invisible to any reader not running a parser. Each witness inhabits a distinct typographic and sensory register — the attendant's warm Cormorant Garamond serif observing through glass, the woman's italic serif feeling proximity and machine heat, the dashcam's IBM Plex Mono logging SPLASH and METALLIC impact — and the design's near-black background with text that emerges from darkness enacts the piece's thesis: meaning assembles from what each account cannot say. The timed reveal (stanzas appearing at thresholds across 90 seconds) is structurally apt, though the piece is strong enough that the "Reveal All" button will be most readers' first click. The overlay's structural notes — "zero shared words," "the shape of an absence" — are the only moment that approaches over-explanation; the artifact might be stronger without them. But this is a minor note against a piece that achieves something genuinely difficult: three poems that are individually compelling, collectively evidence, and whose gaps are more legible than their contents.
