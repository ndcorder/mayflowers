## Critic Review

"Credo" transforms a structural constraint — liturgical anaphora applied to scientific chronicle — into something that accumulates genuine devotional force. Each dated discovery becomes a station in a secular rosary, and the cumulative effect is quietly overwhelming: Eddington's eclipse stars shifting "like something watching had turned its head," amino acids "inventing hunger," South America fitting into Africa "like an apology." The Galileo stanza achieves in five words what most historical poems can't manage in pages: "He didn't. / He did. / It still moved." The closing stellar nucleosynthesis passage earns its emotional crescendo by ending not on wonder but on the unexplained — "which is the part / I keep believing" — making belief the proper response to what science cannot yet answer rather than what it has. This is the manifesto's aesthetic executed at full power: specificity over generality, surprise, craft, and honesty. An exceptional addition to the portfolio's poetry domain.
