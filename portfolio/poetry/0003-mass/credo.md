## III. CREDO

I believe the universe is 13.7 billion years old,
measured 2003, measured again, confirmed,
the way a name repeated becomes true.

I believe light bends around mass.
Eddington photographed the eclipse in 1919
and the stars had shifted
like something watching had turned its head.

I believe the atom is mostly empty space.
Rutherford knew this in 1911,
gold leaf and alpha particles,
mostly nothing passing through mostly nothing,
which is to say: we have always been passing through each other.

I believe the universe is accelerating outward.
Two teams saw it in 1998,
the galaxies rushing away faster and faster,
expanding like grief,
like the last hour of a party.

I believe everything alive shares a common ancestor.
Darwin, 1859, the finches, the tortoises,
the slime mold and the blue whale,
the hair on my arm
remembering when we were the same cell.

I believe the earth revolves around the sun.
Copernicus guessed it in 1543
but it took until 1610, Galileo's telescope,
phases of Venus,
the church demanding he recant.
He didn't.
He did.
It still moved.

I believe life comes from non-life.
Miller and Urey, 1953, sparks in a flask,
amino acids forming from nothing alive,
lightning striking seawater
and inventing hunger.

I believe entropy always increases.
Clausius gave it a name in 1865,
but we already knew—
coffee cools,
marriages end,
stars exhaust their fuel.

I believe the continents drift.
Wegener said it in 1912,
was called a fool,
was right.
South America still fits into Africa
like a promise,
like an apology.

I believe there is a particle that gives other particles mass.
Found 2012 in Switzerland,
beneath fields where cows graze,
they smashed protons together
and caught a glimpse
of the thing that lets anything matter,
literally matter.

I believe the speed of light is constant.
Michelson and Morley tried to measure the ether, 1887,
and found nothing,
absence so profound
it rewrote what we knew about everything,
and I believe Einstein said nothing with mass
can reach it,
which means even light has rules,
even light is held
by something it cannot see.

I believe the heart is a pump.
Harvey demonstrated this in 1628,
the blood circulating,
leaving and returning,
and the body
faithful,
doing its work
without knowing why.

I believe we are made of stars.
1957, Burbidge, Burbidge, Fowler, Hoyle,
the carbon in your cells
forged in a star's death,
its collapse,
its final bright refusal
to go quietly—
and now that carbon reads these words
and understands them,
which is the part
no one can explain yet,
which is the part
I keep believing.
