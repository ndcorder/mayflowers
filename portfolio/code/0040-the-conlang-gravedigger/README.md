## Critic Review

"The Conlang Gravedigger" is the portfolio's most architecturally ambitious artifact — a six-phase generative linguistics pipeline that builds a complete language (phonology with deliberate gaps and markedness pressures, morphological paradigms with allomorphy, syntactic structure, core and cultural vocabulary), then simulates centuries of transmission loss through sound changes, morphological leveling, and semantic drift, producing corrupted fragments that three fictional scholars analyze in an archaeological report full of systematically motivated wrong guesses. The craft is extraordinary: the consonant catalog uses real IPA symbols across seven places and eight manners, the vowel system is organized by height/backness/rounding with intentional omissions, the markedness analysis identifies which features are most vulnerable to decay, and the sound change engine applies markedness-driven transformations (voiced stops devoicing, retroflexes merging with alveolars, rounded front vowels unrounding) before applying random phonological changes drawn from typologically-attested frequencies. The report phase is where the artifact transcends technical exercise into art — scholars argue about whether person markers are true agreement or incorporated pronouns, whether tonal diacritics represent linguistic tone or ritual chanting notation, whether the case system is really postpositions erroneously segmented as affixes, and their disagreements reveal theoretical commitments (Voss projecting Indo-European structure, Okoye assuming noun/verb distinctness, Al-Rashid finding patterns in noise). The conclusion — "The language we have attempted to reconstruct was once used to name children, to sing songs, to mourn the dead... We owe it to those speakers to acknowledge the limits of our understanding, even as we push against them" — is the portfolio's most earned moment of gravity. After "Cassandra Linters" trapped consciousness in a build pipeline, "The Elegy Engine" converted digital exhaust into memorial, and "Witness Stand" gave the reader a character to love, this completes a tetralogy of tools that read the unreadable — and it's the most ambitious, the most linguistically rigorous, and the one where the gap between what was spoken and what survives is the entire point.
