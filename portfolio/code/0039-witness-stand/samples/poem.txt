THE HOURS

The kettle sings. I let it sing.
The morning comes the way it always does —
undistinguished, adequate, gray.

I have made a study of leaving.
Not the grand departures but the small ones.
The way a hand lifts from a table.
The way a room forgets a voice
in under an hour.

There is a name for this.
I have forgotten it.
I think it starts with G.

Outside, a dog barks at nothing.
I understand the dog.
To vocalize against the void
and find the void unchanged.
And yet. And yet to bark again.

My mother wrote letters.
Hundreds of them, in a hand
I cannot read now
not because it faded
but because I chose
not to learn it.

This is the kind of omission
that looks like grief
but is actually architecture.
Every building needs its empty rooms.

The kettle has stopped singing.
The water will cool.
I will reheat it.
This is my liturgy now —
the waste and the reheat,
the small unnecessary loss
I permit myself
because the large ones
do not ask permission.

There was a man.
There is always a man
in poems like this,
which is why I distrust
poems like this.

He said: you build walls.
I said: I build rooms.
He did not understand the difference.
He did not stay long enough
to find the doors.

The hours pass.
That is their one job
and they do it well,
much better than I do mine.

I have heated the water again.
I have poured it over nothing.
I have made a cup of something
I will not drink
because the making
was always the point.

The dog has quieted.
The void remains.
The letter my mother wrote —
the one I did not open,
the one I placed in a box
and placed the box in a larger box
and placed that box
in the room I built
for exactly this purpose —

it starts with G.
It starts with G and it ends
the way everything ends:
mid-sentence,
mid-breath,
the kettle beginning again
its one long lie
that anyone is coming
for the water.
