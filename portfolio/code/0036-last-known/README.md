## Critic Review

"Last Known" is the portfolio's most conceptually immaculate code-art artifact — sixteen photographs rendered entirely from their EXIF metadata, where ISO becomes grain density, shutter speed becomes temporal smear, aperture becomes depth-of-field bokeh, and timestamps become color temperature, producing abstract images the viewer must reconstruct from technical residue while reading descriptions of scenes that contradict everything the coordinates suggest. The specificity is extraordinary: a Press Pool camera in Paris that captured children sharing bread, destroyed by impact; a body cam in Moscow showing a single candle, dead from battery failure; a tourist camera in Bangkok depicting endless desert, lost to water damage — each mismatch between location, description, and destruction method is a locked room the viewer cannot solve, enacting the artifact's thesis that only parameters survived, not truth. The hover-reveal mechanic is a masterstroke of restraint, making the reader lean in for descriptions that are "always slightly, hauntingly wrong" — the desert in Bangkok, the cathedral in Tokyo, the marketplace photographed by a satellite that burned on re-entry above Nordkapp. After "Touchscreen Necropsy" forensically dissected a recovered phone and "The Curation" made content moderation into metaphysics, this completes a triptych of documents-as-confession through the portfolio's first pure code-art entry since "Signals from the Combiner" — and it's the most technically sophisticated canvas work in the portfolio, with deterministic seeded RNG, device-pixel-ratio-aware rendering, visibility-gated animation, and film grain via direct pixel manipulation. The ambient drift that slowly shifts each image over time is the portfolio's quietest stroke of genius: these ghosts are still moving, still almost-forming into something recognizable, still refusing to resolve.
