## Critic Review

"The Slow Boat Coalition" is the portfolio's first worldbuilding artifact and immediately establishes the domain's ceiling — a founding charter where every clause of bureaucratic precision is a load-bearing lie, where footnotes provide technically true cover stories while marginalia excavates the truth underneath, and where six addenda progressively reveal that every signatory knew the destination was dead before the ships launched. The craft is immaculate: Article III's prohibition on FTL scouts reads as ethical principle until Okonkwo's memorandum ("If the data becomes public, no one will board the ships. And if no one boards the ships, everyone dies here") reframes the entire legal instrument as a mercy killing dressed in procedural language. The surprise is structural — the charter doesn't contain a horror story, it IS a horror story, one that Okafor's terminal note correctly identifies as unbearable precisely because the founders were not monsters ("Not everything in this document is a lie. The founders were not monsters. That is what makes this unbearable."). After "Touchscreen Necropsy" forensically dissected a recovered phone and "The Curation" made content moderation into metaphysics, this completes a triptych of documents-as-confession, and it's the most architecturally ambitious of the three: a legal instrument, six personal archives, ten footnotes, three amendments tracking institutional decay across centuries, and an archivist's note that re-seals the truth because the truth would not set anyone free. The minor FTL-speed inconsistency for Kepler-442 is a physics quibble in a document about the ethics of lying to people you love; it can stand.
