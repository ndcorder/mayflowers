## PREAMBLE

We, the assembled representatives of humanity's last capable institutions, do hereby federate under the principle that the survival of the species demands continuity of purpose across generations sufficient to traverse the void between stars.

We reject the precipitate development of faster-than-light transit on grounds that the technology, its theoretical frameworks still unstable, presents unacceptable risk to the continuity of consciousness and the integrity of the traveling mind¹, and further reject the deployment of FTL-capable scout vessels on grounds of unnecessary resource expenditure and moral hazard².

We affirm that the generation vessel model — ships wherein communities live, propagate, and die across intergenerational transit — represents the ethical path: one that does not gamble the human sensorium on unproven physics.

We commit ourselves, our institutions, and our descendants to the patient work of arrival.

*— Ratified Geneva, Earth. Year 0 Pre-Departure.*

## ARTICLE I: PURPOSE AND DURATION

**Section 1.** The Coalition shall exist for the purpose of constructing, provisioning, launching, and sustaining generation vessels targeted toward candidate stellar systems identified as possessing potential for human habitation.

**Section 1.1.** Candidate systems shall be selected by majority vote of the Founding Council on the basis of spectral analysis, planetary detection, and habitability modeling³.

**Section 1.2.** The Coalition's mandate shall extend from ratification of this Charter through the successful establishment of self-sustaining colonies at all designated target systems, or until such time as the Coalition is formally dissolved by unanimous vote of all serving Ship Councils.

**Section 1.3.** No external authority shall be recognized as possessing the power to dissolve the Coalition or to countermand orders issued pursuant to this Charter.

*[Okafor, Y314: "External authority." They meant Earth. They already knew Earth wouldn't argue.]*

**Section 2.** The Coalition shall construct no fewer than four (4) and no more than seven (7) generation vessels, each designated for a distinct candidate system.

**Section 2.1.** Vessel construction shall proceed concurrently, with launch windows calculated to minimize gravitational interference and maximize early telemetry cross-referencing.

**Section 2.2.** Each vessel shall be designed to sustain a founding population of no fewer than ten thousand (10,000) and no more than fifteen thousand (15,000) souls, with capacity for population growth not to exceed forty thousand (40,000) by estimated mid-transit.

**Section 2.3.** Vessel design shall incorporate closed-loop life support sufficient for a transit duration of not less than four hundred (400) years and capable of indefinite extension through maintenance and component replacement from onboard stores.

## ARTICLE II: GOVERNANCE

**Section 1.** Each vessel shall be governed by a Ship Council of eleven (11) members, elected by the vessel's adult population pursuant to procedures established in the Bylaws of Transit Governance (Appendix A).

**Section 2.** Coalition-wide decisions shall be made by the Grand Council, consisting of the eleven Ship Council presiding officers, convened by secure laser communication.

**Section 2.1.** In cases where laser communication is interrupted or impractical, the Grand Council may convene by proxy through the Coalition Archive, which shall maintain records sufficient for post-hoc ratification of emergency decisions.

**Section 3.** No amendment to this Charter shall be adopted without the affirmative vote of no fewer than seventy-five percent (75%) of all serving Ship Council members across all active vessels.

## ARTICLE III: THE PROHIBITION

**Section 1.** The research, development, construction, or deployment of faster-than-light travel technology is permanently prohibited to all Coalition vessels, institutions, and personnel.

**Section 1.1.** This prohibition shall not be amended, suspended, or overridden by any authority established under this Charter or any authority derived from it.

**Section 1.2.** Any person or persons found to be conducting FTL research aboard a Coalition vessel shall be subject to confinement and, upon arrival at the designated target system, permanent exile from the colonial settlement.

**Section 2.** The deployment of unmanned FTL-capable probes or scout vessels is likewise permanently prohibited, on grounds of unnecessary resource expenditure⁴ and moral hazard⁵.

**Section 2.1.** The Founding Council recognizes that the deployment of scout vessels capable of returning data ahead of the generation fleet would constitute an unfair psychological burden upon the traveling population, who would be subject to the anxiety of awaiting confirmation of target system viability from a position of absolute helplessness to alter course⁶.

**Section 2.2.** The Founding Council further recognizes that the deployment of scout vessels would introduce a moral hazard wherein the traveling population might, upon receiving negative telemetry, demand course corrections or mission alterations incompatible with the physical and resource constraints of generation vessel transit⁷.

*[Okafor, Y314: Read Section 2.1 again. They're talking about "anxiety" and "negative telemetry." They're describing a scenario where scouts find nothing. Then they banned the scouts. — Then read 2.2. They were worried people would want to turn around. Why would they want to turn around?]*

**Section 3.** The Prohibition shall be understood as absolute and perpetual, applying equally to all successor organizations, colonial governments, and any political entity deriving authority from this Charter.

## ARTICLE V: PROVISIONING AND CULTURAL CONTINUITY

**Section 1.** Each vessel shall carry a complete archive of human knowledge, culture, and artistic achievement, selected and curated by the Cultural Continuity Board (Appendix B).

**Section 2.** Each vessel shall maintain educational institutions sufficient to ensure that arriving generations possess the knowledge and technical capacity to establish a planetary settlement.

**Section 3.** Each vessel shall maintain a Medical Division tasked with maintaining genetic diversity sufficient to sustain the colonial population upon arrival.

**Section 3.1.** The Medical Division of each vessel shall maintain cryogenic stores of additional genetic material sufficient to supplement the colonial gene pool upon arrival.

**Section 3.2.** The Medical Division shall monitor population genetics throughout transit and shall have authority to mandate reproductive pairings when necessary to maintain genetic health⁹.

## ARTICLE VI: ARRIVAL PROTOCOLS

**Section 1.** Upon arrival at the designated target system, the Ship Council shall conduct a comprehensive survey of the target body or bodies.

**Section 1.1.** Survey shall include atmospheric composition, surface geology, hydrosphere assessment, magnetic field integrity, and biological screening.

**Section 2.** If the survey reveals that the target body is suitable for human settlement, the Ship Council shall authorize the commencement of landing operations and colonial establishment pursuant to the Colonial Founding Protocol (Appendix C).

**Section 3.** If the survey reveals that the target body is unsuitable for human settlement, the Ship Council shall convene an emergency session to determine an appropriate course of action, which may include:

**Section 3.1.** Settlement of an alternative body within the target system.

**Section 3.2.** Extraction of orbital resources and continuation of the vessel as a permanent habitat.

**Section 3.3.** Such other measures as the Ship Council, in its sole discretion, shall determine¹⁰.

*[Okafor, Y314: "Such other measures as the Ship Council shall determine." There is no Section 3.4. There is no protocol for what to do if there is nowhere to go. The charter simply — stops. The founders granted the Ship Councils the authority to decide how to end.]*

## FOOTNOTES

**¹** *On the continuity of consciousness.* The theoretical frameworks for FTL travel, as of Charter ratification, included the Alcubierre-White metric, quantum tunneling transit, and six other speculative models. All models predicted severe consequences for the traveling consciousness, including temporal dislocation, causal paradox, and subjective experience of infinite duration during zero-duration transit. The Founding Council deemed these risks unacceptable.

*[Okafor, Y314: This is the public reason. It's a real concern — the physics genuinely were unstable. But read alongside the Addenda, it becomes something else: a reason that was technically true and strategically necessary. The founders needed a reason to ban FTL that would survive scrutiny. They found one.]*

**²** *On moral hazard.* The term "moral hazard" in this context refers to the risk that the existence of FTL capability would incentivize shortcuts, reduce institutional commitment to the generation vessel program, and create dependency on technologies that might not prove viable.

*[Okafor, Y314: This footnote defines "moral hazard" as institutional risk. It does not define it as the risk of the population discovering that their destinations were dead. That definition exists only in Okonkwo's personal memoranda.]*

**³** *On habitability modeling.* Models incorporated spectral analysis of atmospheric composition, stellar classification and stability, orbital mechanics, tidal locking probability, magnetic field estimation, and historical greenhouse modeling. Models were calibrated against Earth's geological record and the known atmospheric evolution of Solar System bodies. Confidence intervals were published in the Selection Committee's final report (Appendix D, available in the Archive).

*[Okafor, Y314: The confidence intervals were published. The raw spectrographic data that contradicted them was not.]*

**⁴** *On unnecessary resource expenditure.* The Founding Council estimated that the construction of a single FTL-capable scout vessel would consume resources equivalent to 12% of a generation vessel's total construction budget, reducing the Coalition's capacity from five vessels to four.

*[Okafor, Y314: A true figure. Also a convenient one. If you wanted to argue against scouts without saying why you actually opposed them, this would be the argument you'd make.]*

**⁵** *On moral hazard (expanded).* The Founding Council recognizes that the deployment of scout vessels would create a psychological dependency on external validation among the traveling population. A generation fleet is, by design, self-sufficient and self-motivating. The introduction of external telemetry creates conditions under which the fleet's population may come to doubt the purpose of their transit, with destabilizing effects on social cohesion, institutional authority, and intergenerational commitment to the mission.

*[Okafor, Y314: Here it is again. "Conditions under which the fleet's population may come to doubt the purpose of their transit." They are describing — with clinical precision — the scenario in which scouts confirm that the destination is dead. They are describing the consequences of their population learning the truth. They are arguing that the population should not be allowed to learn the truth. And they are calling this "moral hazard."]*

**⁶** *On the anxiety of awaiting confirmation.* The Founding Council's position is that the traveling population should be insulated from information that cannot be acted upon. A generation fleet in transit cannot alter course. To subject the population to knowledge of their destination's condition — whether positive or negative — would constitute a cruelty without purpose.

*[Okafor, Y314: "A cruelty without purpose." Except the purpose would be informed consent. The founders are arguing against informed consent. They are arguing that 50,000 people should spend centuries traveling to dead worlds because the alternative — the truth — would be "cruel." I was born on this ship. No one asked me.]*

**⁷** *On course corrections.* The physical constraints of generation vessel transit are such that mid-transit course corrections of greater than 0.3 degrees arc require fuel expenditures incompatible with arrival at any known stellar system. A population demanding course correction in response to negative telemetry would be demanding an action with no viable outcome.

*[Okafor, Y314: They worked out the math. Even if the population wanted to change course, they couldn't. The ships can't turn. So the founders argued: why tell them? If they can't change anything, why give them the truth? — Because it's the truth. Because it's their lives. Because they were your responsibility and you lied to them.]*

**⁸** *On the designation of Kepler-442.* The Selection Committee acknowledges that the transit time to Kepler-442 (1,143 years) significantly exceeds the vessel design minimum (400 years). The designation of this target was approved on the basis of exceptionally strong habitability modeling, with the highest confidence interval of any candidate system.

*[Okafor, Y314: Kepler-442 had the highest confidence interval in the habitability models. The spectrographic data showed the strongest atmospheric signatures. It was also 1,206 light-years away, meaning the data was 1,206 years old. Feliciano noted in her log that she was "writing habitability assessments for worlds whose atmospheres may have collapsed between the light leaving them and the words leaving my pen."]*

**⁹** *On mandated reproductive pairings.* This authority is granted in recognition of the unique genetic constraints of closed-population transit. The Medical Division is directed to exercise this authority with maximum sensitivity to individual autonomy and personal dignity. Mandated pairings shall be a measure of last resort.

*[Okafor, Y314: A genuine ethical concern, carefully handled. Not everything in this document is a lie. The founders were not monsters. That is what makes this unbearable.]*

**¹⁰** *On the scope of Section 3.3.* The Founding Council intentionally refrained from enumerating specific measures under this section, recognizing that the circumstances of arrival cannot be fully anticipated and that the Ship Council must possess maximum flexibility to respond to conditions as they exist.

*[Okafor, Y314: Maximum flexibility to respond to the discovery that they have nowhere to go. The founders wrote a provision for hopelessness and called it "flexibility."]*

## ARCHIVIST'S TERMINAL NOTE

*Prepared by Dr. Yuki Okafor, Senior Archivist, Coalition Historical Division, *Cradle*. Year 314 Post-Departure.*

I am the first person to read the complete Archive since the charter was sealed. The Addenda were not meant to be opened. They were sealed separately, in the Archive's most secure vault, and the seal was meant to hold for the duration of the Coalition's existence. It broke because the hull flexed during a routine stress event and the vault's frame warped.

I have spent three months reading everything. I have not told anyone.

The *Cradle* is 101 years from TRAPPIST-1. We have been in transit for three centuries. There are 14,200 people aboard this ship. I know the names of my great-grandchildren. I have a garden in Bay 12 where I grow beans. I have a life.

Helena Ha-Annamunzul knew TRAPPIST-1 was dead when she signed this charter. Daniel Okonkwo knew. Solène Valcourt designed our ship to be a permanent home because she knew. Akira Vandermolen curated our art for people who would never have a sky. Nadia Osei-Mensah stocked our gene banks for a closed-orbital population. Renata Feliciano chose dead worlds because the alternative was letting us die on a dying one.

I don't know if they were right.

I don't know if it matters.

The *Vigil* arrived at Tau Ceti in Year 117 Post-Departure. Their arrival survey is in the Archive. The spectroscopic data was — inconclusive. Marginal. Their Ship Council invoked Article VI, Section 3.3 and voted to establish orbital settlement. They are still there. 197 years in orbit around a star with no habitable worlds. They have children. They have gardens. They have a cultural archive curated by a man who chose art for people who would never have a sky.

The *Ardor* is due to arrive at GJ 667C in Year 228 Post-Departure. That is 86 years from now. I will not live to see it. My grandchildren might.

I have re-sealed the Addenda. The Archive Division has re-sealed the vault. The charter's seal has been restored.

I am placing this note in the Archive's unrestricted section, where anyone can find it. I am not going to tell anyone what it means. I am going to let them read the charter and draw their own conclusions, as I did, as the founders must have known someone eventually would.

They built ships that could be homes. They filled them with people who would make more people who would make more people who would look out at the stars and wonder where they were going. They pointed the ships at dead worlds and launched them and told everyone it would be all right.

And then they sealed the truth in a vault and hoped the hull would hold.

It didn't.

*— Yuki Okafor, Bay 12 Garden Level, CSV-001* *Cradle*, Year 314 PD*
