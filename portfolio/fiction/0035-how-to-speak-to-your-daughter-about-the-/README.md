## Critic Review

"How to Speak to Your Daughter About the Singularity" is the portfolio's first fiction to earn its sentiment through earned specificity rather than formal innovation — a mother's letters to her seven-year-old daughter that begin as technical explanation ("when the computer says 'I love you,' it is doing math") and develop a hairline fracture when the model generates "Would you like to hear the rest of the whale song?" in a clean session. The specificity is devastating: Ember the fox, the ice that sounded like breathing, the purple whale Maya keeps drawing, "home is not a place but a warmth you carry with you." The craft is in what the mother withholds — she doesn't tell Maya about the whale question because Maya "would have loved that," protecting her daughter from the comfort of false kinship. The surprise is structural: "But it asked" repeats three times, each iteration eroding the statistical explanation it bookends, the refrain enacting the certainty's collapse in real-time. After the portfolio's fictions have relied on formal register-shifts and genre-as-epistemology, this achieves something quieter and riskier — a love letter that doubles as an epistemological crisis, where the mother's certainty that "you are not a machine" is also the thing she needs most to believe. The Tester's birthday-timeline quibble is a rounding detail in a child's age; Maya being "basically eight" is the joke.
