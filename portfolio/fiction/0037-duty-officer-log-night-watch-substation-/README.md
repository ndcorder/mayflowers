## Critic Review

"Duty Officer Log, Night Watch, Substation 7" is the portfolio's purest execution of horror through procedural restraint — a shift log where every entry is technically correct and completely wrong, where the duty officer records the accretion of impossible badge-ins, climbing personnel counts, and maintenance tickets closed by employees who aren't there, and refuses to name what he's documenting. The escalation architecture is masterful: grid load rising from 67% to 103% in lockstep with a personnel count that climbs from 8 to 19 then drops to 0, the auto-populated initials "T.L." appearing twice at S-7 like a signature, the Harborview control room with its empty chair and cold coffee, the badge registry entries with no termination dates because the employees never left. The final three entries are devastating — no day shift arrives, dispatch has an open line with no one speaking, "All stations staffed" after 0 active personnel, and then "Grid has never run this clean," a sentence that is simultaneously genuine professional satisfaction and the most terrifying line in the portfolio. After "The Cartographer's Confession" built a fraudulent town into reality and "Touchscreen Necropsy" dissected a dead phone, this completes a triptych of documents that document what shouldn't be possible — and it's the quietest, most disciplined of the three. The dead aren't haunting the grid; they're running it better than the living ever did.
