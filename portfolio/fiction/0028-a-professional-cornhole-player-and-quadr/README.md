## Critic Review

"Cornhole Player and Quadruple Amputee Arrested for Murder" is the portfolio's most structurally ambitious fiction since "Mise en Abyme" — a single event refracted through six distinct narrative registers (wire service, Facebook thread, true crime podcast, Wikipedia edit war, letter to the editor, fourth-grader's book report), where guilt and innocence shift depending on which genre is doing the telling, and where the NPR headline embedded as the HTML title hangs over everything as a comment noting "It wasn't the story." The specificity is extraordinary: Tasha Gentry who never gets to speak on the podcast, the twenty-minute silence between the argument and the sirens, the myoelectric prosthetics with "proportional grip control," the Wikipedia editor who changes "convicted murderer" to "man awaiting trial" before a verdict exists, the sister's observation that the world has a Dale Ketterly story and not a Danny Mercer story. The surprise is the book report arriving last and cutting closest — "I think Danny Mercer probably had a favorite color and a favorite food and nobody even asked" — a fourth-grader accidentally articulating what none of the professional narrators could. After 18 artifacts with zero humor, the Facebook thread is the funniest sustained passage in the portfolio (Dawn Adkins: "Did he strangle Danny with his mind?"; Mrs. Henderson's A- for starting a sentence with "also"), and the humor makes the sister's letter and the child's final line land harder. Each CSS style is meticulously differentiated — Comic Sans for the report, dark theme for the podcast, diff-colored Wikipedia, cream stationery with gold border for the letter — and every technical detail serves the thesis: narrative register is not neutral, genre is not transparent, and the most reliable witness is the one who doesn't know enough to distort.
