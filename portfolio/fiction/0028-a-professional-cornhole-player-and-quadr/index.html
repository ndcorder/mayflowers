BULLITT COUNTY, Ky. —

Authorities arrested Dale Ketterly, 34, on Saturday in connection with the death of Danny Mercer, whose body was discovered Thursday near a cornhole tournament venue outside Shepherdsville.

Ketterly, a ranked competitor on the American Cornhole League who lost all four limbs in a 2019 industrial accident, was taken into custody at his home without incident. He is being held at the Bullitt County Detention Center on one count of murder, according to court records. A bond hearing is scheduled for Monday.

Kentucky State Police said Mercer's body was found in a wooded area behind the Bullitt County Fairgrounds shortly after 8 p.m. Thursday. A cause of death has not been released pending an autopsy. Trooper Jacob Hall said investigators were "following all available leads" but declined to provide further details, citing the ongoing investigation.

"We understand there is significant public interest in this case, and we ask for patience as we work to determine exactly what happened," Hall said in a written statement.

The ACL released a statement Saturday expressing shock and offering condolences to Mercer's family. "We are deeply saddened by this tragedy and will cooperate fully with law enforcement," the organization said.

—Associated Press

Letters to the Editor
The truth about Danny Mercer

To the Editor:

I have read your coverage of my brother's death with a feeling I can only describe as nausea.

Danny Mercer was my brother. He was not a "competitor." He was not a "rival." He was a person who made everyone around him feel lighter. He coached Little League. He brought my daughter a balloon animal from the county fair every single year, even after she stopped believing they were made by magic. He was the kind of person who remembered your birthday even if you'd only met once.

The story everyone wants to tell is about Dale Ketterly. The quadruple amputee. The cornhole player with the incredible comeback. That's the interesting story, isn't it? That's the one that gets the clicks and the podcast episodes and the Wikipedia page. Nobody wants to write about who Danny was. Nobody wants to know that he carried a photo of our mother in his wallet every day since she passed, or that he cried at every graduation he attended because he was so proud of people he barely knew.

I don't know what happened that night. I wasn't there. But I know that my brother is dead, and that Dale Ketterly was there, and that someone is responsible. And I know that no matter what the courts decide, I will spend the rest of my life knowing that the world has a Dale Ketterly story and not a Danny Mercer story, and that seems like the cruelest thing of all.

Sincerely,
Karen Mercer-Williams
Louisville
