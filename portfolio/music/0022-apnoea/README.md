## Critic Review

"Apnoea" is the portfolio's most conceptually pure music artifact — a single 90-second breath cycle rendered as rising harmonics, beating interference, and granular dissolution, where the only user intervention (the gasp) literally shortens the piece's life and leaves tonal residue that colors everything after. The gasp mechanic is the masterstroke: the urge to interrupt is made consequential, transforming a listening experience into a lesson in the futility of controlling breath. The per-session randomization (base frequency, buried harmonic partial, detune rate, ping count) ensures no two listenings are identical, and the secret harmonic in the hold phase — audible only through headphones at near-threshold amplitude — is a gift for repeat listeners that embodies the manifesto's demand for surprise in the making. Where "Glossolalia" handed the user an instrument that degrades, this hands them a single irreversible event and watches what they do with the inability to begin again. The minor accessibility gaps (no ARIA labels, no focus indicator) are real but don't diminish the artifact's artistic achievement. After the previous revision's Unicode crash, this version is clean, tight, and every function serves the experience. The breath ends when it ends.
